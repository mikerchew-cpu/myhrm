# Malaysia Employment Act 1955 — Complete Reference

**Department:** HR

---

## Overview

The Employment Act 1955 (Akta Kerja 1955) is Malaysia's primary labour legislation. Effective **1 January 2023**, the Act was substantially amended to extend coverage to **ALL employees** regardless of wage level (previously capped at RM2,000/month). The 2023 amendments also introduced new protections including paternity leave, flexible work arrangements, and a ban on forced labour.

---

## Working Hours

| Provision | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Maximum daily hours | 8 hours per day |
| Maximum weekly hours | 45 hours per week |
| Spread-over period | Not more than 10 hours per day |
| Break | 30 minutes after 5 consecutive hours of work |
| Continuous work (exemption) | Can be reduced to 6 hours/day if OT expected |

**Key Points:**
- An employee cannot work more than 5 consecutive hours without a 30-minute break.
- The spread-over period (time from start to end of work including breaks) cannot exceed 10 hours.
- Different industries may have sector-specific orders modifying these hours.
- Any work beyond 8 hours/day or 45 hours/week constitutes overtime.

---

## Rest Days

- **Minimum:** 1 rest day per week (normally Sunday, but can be any day as agreed).
- If the employee is required to work on a rest day, overtime rates apply.

| Scenario | OT Rate |
|---|---|
| Worked ≤ half normal working hours | 0.5 × ORP |
| Worked > half normal working hours | 1.0 × ORP |
| Worked exceeding normal working hours | 2.0 × HRP |

**ORP** = Ordinary Rate of Pay (Monthly Salary ÷ 26)  
**HRP** = Hourly Rate of Pay (ORP ÷ Normal Daily Hours)

---

## Public Holidays

- **11 paid public holidays** per year (including 5 gazetted: National Day, Agong's Birthday, Yang di-Pertua Negeri birthdays, Malaysia Day, Labour Day; and 6 others declared by the Federal/State Government).
- Employer may substitute up to 4 of the 11 holidays with other public holidays.
- Holiday falling on a rest day is observed on the following working day.

| Scenario | Rate |
|---|---|
| Work on PH (normal working hours) | 2.0 × ORP |
| Work on PH (beyond normal hours) | 3.0 × HRP |

---

## Annual Leave

| Length of Service | Entitlement |
|---|---|
| 1–2 years | 8 days |
| 2–5 years | 12 days |
| 5+ years | 16 days |

**Rules:**
- Pro-rated for incomplete years of service.
- Unused annual leave may be carried forward as per company policy (but forfeited after 12 months if not taken).
- Upon termination, unused annual leave must be paid out (encashment).
- Employer must grant leave within 12 months of entitlement or compensate.
- Sick leave may be deducted from annual leave only if the employee is unfit for work with a medical certificate (per company policy).

---

## Sick Leave

| Length of Service | Non-hospitalised (per year) |
|---|---|
| < 2 years | 14 days |
| 2–5 years | 18 days |
| 5+ years | 22 days |

**Hospitalisation:** 60 days (separate pool since 2023 amendment).  
**Total combined cap:** Non-hospitalised + hospitalisation cannot exceed 60 days total per year (but hospitalisation is drawn first from its own pool of 60).

**Requirements:**
- Medical certificate from a registered medical practitioner or dentist.
- Employer may require examination by its own doctor (at employer's cost).
- Sick leave is paid at the ORP.
- Uncertified sick leave may be treated as unpaid leave at employer's discretion.

---

## Maternity Leave

- **98 consecutive days** (up from 60 days under the pre-2023 Act).
- Paid at the ORP.
- Eligible after 90 days of service in the 9 months before confinement.
- **Exclusion:** Women with 5 or more surviving children at the time of confinement.
- Employer **cannot terminate** an employee while she is pregnant (or during maternity leave) except for specific misconduct.
- Early return to work requires employer **consent** and a **medical certificate** certifying fitness.
- Maternity allowance must be paid regardless of whether the employee intends to return to work.

---

## Paternity Leave

- **7 consecutive days** per childbirth.
- Entitled to married male employees.
- Limited to 7 days per confinement regardless of number of children.
- Must give notice to employer as soon as practicable.
- Paid at ordinary rate of pay.

---

## Termination Notice & Payment in Lieu

| Length of Service | Notice Period |
|---|---|
| < 2 years | 4 weeks |
| 2–5 years | 6 weeks |
| 5+ years | 8 weeks |

(Applicable to both employer and employee unless contract provides more favourable terms.)

- **Payment in Lieu:** Employer may terminate immediately by paying salary equivalent to the notice period.
- **Summary dismissal:** Employer may dismiss without notice for misconduct (after due inquiry).
- **Constructive dismissal:** Employee may resign without notice if employer breaches contract terms.
- Notice must be **in writing**.

**Probation Period Notice:**
- Per contract (typically 1–2 weeks).
- If unspecified, the statutory minimum applies.

---

## Termination Benefits (Retirement/Retrenchment)

| Length of Service | Benefit Per Year of Service |
|---|---|
| < 2 years | 10 days' wages |
| 2–5 years | 15 days' wages |
| 5+ years | 20 days' wages |

**Applies to:**
- Employees earning **≤ RM4,000/month** (any occupation)
- Employees engaged in **manual labour** (regardless of wage)
- Applicable to retrenchment, voluntary separation scheme (VSS), retirement, and similar.

**Formula:** (Monthly Salary ÷ 26) × Entitlement Days × Years of Service

---

## Flexible Work Arrangement (FWA)

- Employees may **apply in writing** to vary hours, days, or place of work.
- Employer must **respond in writing within 60 days** of receiving the application.
- Grounds for refusal must be provided.
- Introduced as a new right under the 2023 amendments.
- Employees may make one application per year (unless circumstances change).

---

## Sexual Harassment

- Employer **must exhibit** an anti-sexual harassment notice in the workplace.
- Mandatory requirement since **1 January 2023**.
- Complaints must be investigated and resolved.
- Employer must protect complainants from victimisation.

---

## Forced Labour & Discrimination

- **Forced labour is prohibited** — no employee shall be required to perform involuntary work.
- **Discrimination in employment is prohibited** — includes discrimination based on race, religion, gender, nationality, or physical disability in hiring, promotion, or termination.
- These provisions were strengthened under the 2023 amendments.

---

## Record Keeping

- Maintain employment records for **6 years**.
- Records must include: personal details, employment contract, working hours, leave, wages, deductions, termination details.
- **Itemised payslip** mandatory since **1 January 2023**:
  - Full name, EPF/SOCSO/EIS numbers
  - Basic salary, allowances, OT, bonus, deductions, net pay
  - Contributions by employer

---

## Penalties

| Offence | Maximum Penalty |
|---|---|
| General penalty | Up to **RM50,000** |
| Failure to maintain records | RM10,000 – RM50,000 |
| Failure to provide payslip | Up to RM50,000 |
| Forced labour violations | Higher penalties per amendment |
