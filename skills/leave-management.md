# Malaysia Leave Management — Complete Policies

**Department:** HR

---

## Overview

The Employment Act 1955 prescribes minimum leave entitlements for all employees in Malaysia. This guide covers annual leave, sick leave, maternity leave, paternity leave, public holidays, unpaid leave, half-day leave, and the application process, along with best practices for HR administration.

---

## Annual Leave

### Statutory Entitlement

| Length of Service | Annual Leave Entitlement |
|---|---|
| 1–2 years | 8 days per year |
| 2–5 years | 12 days per year |
| 5+ years | 16 days per year |

- Pro-rated for an incomplete year of service.
- More favourable leave may be provided in the employment contract.

### Carry-Forward Rules

- Unused annual leave may be carried forward to the next year subject to company policy.
- The employee **forfeits** the right to annual leave if not taken **within 12 months** from the date of entitlement.
- Employers may impose a "use it or lose it" policy but must allow for reasonable scheduling.

### Encashment on Termination

- Upon termination (resignation, retrenchment, dismissal, or death), all **unused annual leave must be encashed**.
- Encashment rate = (Monthly Salary ÷ 26) × Number of unused days.
- If the employee was on notice period, the employer may require the employee to clear leave during the notice period (subject to operational requirements).

### Encashment While Employed (Optional)

- Some companies allow encashment of accumulated leave beyond a certain cap (e.g., > 30 days).
- This is not a statutory requirement — it is purely per company policy.

### Pro-Rata Calculation

For incomplete year of service:

> Pro-rata leave = (Annual Entitlement ÷ 12) × Completed months of service

Round to the nearest half or full day as per company policy.

## Sick Leave

### Statutory Entitlement

| Length of Service | Non-Hospitalised Sick Leave (per year) |
|---|---|
| < 2 years | 14 days |
| 2–5 years | 18 days |
| 5+ years | 22 days |

**Hospitalisation Leave:** 60 days per year (separate pool since the 2023 amendment).

### Interaction Between Pools

| Scenario | Rule |
|---|---|
| Employee takes hospitalisation leave | Drawn from the 60-day hospitalisation pool |
| Employee takes non-hospitalisation sick leave | Drawn from the 14/18/22-day pool |
| Combined total | Cannot exceed 60 days total per year |
| Example: 10 days non-hospitalised + 40 days hospitalised | 50 days used; remaining 10 days available from hospitalisation pool |

### Medical Certificate Requirement

- A **medical certificate (MC)** from a registered medical practitioner or dentist is required.
- Employer may require the employee to be examined by a company-appointed doctor (at the employer's cost).
- If the employee refuses the company doctor's examination, sick leave may be treated as unpaid.

### Paid Sick Leave Rate

- Sick leave is paid at the **Ordinary Rate of Pay (ORP)** = Monthly Salary ÷ 26.

### Uncertified Sick Leave

- Days taken off for illness without an MC may be treated as **unpaid leave** or deducted from annual leave, at the employer's discretion (per company policy).

## Maternity Leave

### Entitlement

- **98 consecutive days** of paid maternity leave.
- Eligible after at least **90 days of service** with the employer in the 9 months before confinement.

### Maternity Allowance

- Paid at the **Ordinary Rate of Pay**.
- Must be paid **regardless of whether the employee intends to return to work**.

### Exclusion

- **Not entitled** if the employee has **5 or more surviving children** at the time of confinement.

### Early Return to Work

- Employee can return early only with:
  - **Employer's written consent**, and
  - A **medical certificate** certifying fitness to work.

### Protection Against Dismissal

- Employer **cannot terminate** an employee:
  - While she is pregnant
  - During maternity leave
  - Due to pregnancy-related illness
- Exception: Termination for **willful breach of duty** or **serious misconduct** (after due inquiry).

### Maternity Leave Policy Best Practices

- Require written notice of pregnancy and expected due date.
- Offer additional paid/non-paid leave beyond statutory (e.g., 120 days).
- Maintain clear records of maternity leave taken and paid.

## Paternity Leave

### Entitlement

- **7 consecutive days** of paid paternity leave.
- Limited to **married male employees**.
- Applicable **per childbirth** (not per child for multiple births).
- Must give notice to employer **as soon as practicable** (preferably in writing before the leave).

### Pay

- Paid at the **Ordinary Rate of Pay** (ORP).
- Not pro-rated for part-time employees (full 7 days as statutory minimum).

### Best Practices

- Require a copy of the marriage certificate and birth certificate.
- Allow paternity leave to be taken flexibly within a defined window (e.g., within 30 days of delivery), though the statutory requirement is consecutive.
- Consider offering additional paternity leave as a company benefit.

## Public Holidays

### Statutory Minimum

- **11 paid gazetted public holidays** per year.
- Employer may substitute up to **4 holidays** with other public holidays (subject to written notice).
- The 5 compulsory public holidays under the Act are:
  1. National Day (31 August)
  2. Agong's Birthday (first Monday of June)
  3. Yang di-Pertua Negeri birthdays (state-specific)
  4. Malaysia Day (16 September)
  5. Labour Day (1 May)

### Public Holiday on a Rest Day

- If a public holiday falls on a rest day, the **following working day** is observed as the paid public holiday.

### Work on a Public Holiday

| Scenario | Rate |
|---|---|
| Worked within normal hours | 2.0 × ORP |
| Worked beyond normal hours | 3.0 × HRP |

## Unpaid Leave

- Approved at the **employer's discretion**.
- Employee must submit a written request in advance.
- **Impact on statutory contributions:**
  - **EPF:** Only payable on actual wages paid for the month. Unpaid days reduce the contribution.
  - **SOCSO:** Based on actual wages paid.
  - **EIS:** Based on actual wages paid.
- Absence without approval may constitute misconduct.
- Unpaid leave beyond a certain threshold may affect continuous service calculation (e.g., for annual leave entitlement).

---

## Half-Day Leave

- Not provided for under the Employment Act — it is a **company policy option**.
- Usually managed by deducting ½ day from annual leave entitlement.
- The employer determines whether half-days are permitted and any minimum notice requirements.
- Some employers allow half-day for personal appointments, medical visits, etc.

## Leave Application Process

### Recommended Workflow

```
Employee submits request (HR system / email / form)
           ↓
Manager reviews and approves/declines
           ↓
HR records and updates leave balance
           ↓
Payroll processes (deductions, encashment)
```

### Required Information

- Leave type (annual, sick, maternity, paternity, unpaid, half-day)
- Dates and time (from–to)
- Reason (optional for annual leave; required for sick/unpaid)
- Medical certificate (if sick leave)
- Supporting documents (marriage/birth certificate for paternity, etc.)

### Notice Period for Annual Leave

- **Recommended:** At least **2 weeks' notice** for leave exceeding 2 days.
- **Minimum:** Per company policy (24–48 hours for single-day leave).
- Employer may refuse leave due to **operational requirements** (with reasonable grounds).

### Sick Leave Notification

- Employee must notify the employer **within 24 hours** (or as specified in the contract) of the inability to attend work.
- Medical certificate must be submitted upon return or within 48 hours.

## Record Keeping

| Requirement | Retention Period |
|---|---|
| Leave records (types, dates, payments) | **6 years** |
| Medical certificates | **6 years** (or per company policy) |
| Leave applications and approvals | **6 years** |
| Maternity/paternity documents | **6 years** |

### Digital Records

- HR systems should maintain an up-to-date leave balance for each employee.
- Employees should have self-service access to view their leave balances.
- Audit trail of leave approvals/rejections should be maintained.

## Policy Design Considerations

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Annual leave exceeding statutory | Competitive benefit, retention tool |
| Annual leave carry-forward cap | Typically 2× annual entitlement |
| Medical certificate threshold | Company policy may require MC from day 1 |
| Hospitalisation leave | Align with statutory (60 days) |
| Bereavement leave | Consider 2–5 days (not statutory) |
| Marriage leave | Consider 2–3 days (not statutory) |
| Study/exam leave | Consider for professional development |
| Compensatory time off | Alternative to OT pay (per company policy) |
