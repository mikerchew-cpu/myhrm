# Performance Management & Talent Matrix Guide

**Department:** HR

---

## Overview

This guide covers performance appraisal cycles, rating scales, KPI setting, the 9-Box Grid talent matrix, talent review and succession planning, performance improvement plans (PIP), rewards, documentation, and legal considerations. It is designed for HR professionals managing performance across the organisation.

---

## Performance Appraisal

### Appraisal Frequency

| Cycle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Annual** | Year-end review covering the full performance period |
| **Bi-annual** | Mid-year + year-end reviews |
| **Quarterly** | Best for fast-paced roles / project-based work |
| **Continuous** | Real-time feedback via check-ins (supplemental to formal review) |

### Recommended Cadence
- **Quarterly check-ins** (informal progress review)
- **Mid-year review** (formal, mid-cycle correction)
- **Year-end review** (full appraisal, rating, and rewards linkage)

### Process Flow

```
Self-Assessment by employee
           ↓
Manager Assessment
           ↓
Calibration across department/company
           ↓
Rating finalisation
           ↓
Appraisal discussion (manager + employee)
           ↓
Goal setting for next period
           ↓
Sign-off by both parties
           ↓
HR records and processes rewards
```

## Rating Scale

### Option 1: Numerical Scale (1–100)

| Range | Rating | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| 90–100 | 5 | Outstanding — Exceeds all expectations consistently |
| 75–89 | 4 | Exceeds Expectations — Regularly exceeds targets |
| 60–74 | 3 | Meets Expectations — Fully competent, delivers on all goals |
| 40–59 | 2 | Below Expectations — Partial achievement, gaps |
| < 40 | 1 | Unsatisfactory — Significant performance gaps |

### Option 2: Simplified 1–5 Scale

| Score | Label | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Outstanding | Top 5% of performers; role model |
| 4 | Exceeds | Performs above requirements consistently |
| 3 | Meets | Fully meets all performance expectations |
| 2 | Below | Some areas not meeting expectations |
| 1 | Unsatisfactory | Consistently failing to meet standards |

### Forced Distribution (Optional)

| Rating | Target % of Employees |
|---|---|
| 5 — Outstanding | 5–10% |
| 4 — Exceeds | 15–20% |
| 3 — Meets | 55–65% |
| 2 — Below | 5–10% |
| 1 — Unsatisfactory | ≤ 5% |

Forced distribution should be used as a **guide** not a strict quota. Calibration helps ensure fairness.

## KPI Setting — SMART Goals

### SMART Criteria

| Letter | Criterion | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | Specific | "Increase customer satisfaction score" (not "Do better") |
| **M** | Measurable | "From 82% to 90%" |
| **A** | Achievable | Realistic given resources and timeframe |
| **R** | Relevant | Aligned with department and company objectives |
| **T** | Time-bound | "By 31 December 2026" |

### Goal Categories

| Category | Weight (Example) |
|---|---|
| **Quantitative KPIs** (revenue, sales, production, SLAs) | 50% |
| **Qualitative KPIs** (quality, customer feedback, process improvement) | 25% |
| **Behavioural / Values** (teamwork, integrity, innovation) | 15% |
| **Development goals** (training, certification, skill building) | 10% |

### Cascading Goals

```
Company Objectives (OKRs / Strategy)
           ↓
Department Objectives
           ↓
Team Objectives
           ↓
Individual KPIs
```

- Each individual KPI should trace back to a department or company objective.
- Quarterly review ensures KPIs remain relevant as business priorities shift.

## 9-Box Grid — Performance vs Potential

### Grid Structure

| Performance \ Potential | Low Potential | Medium Potential | High Potential |
|---|---|---|---|
| **High Performance** | **Box 4** — Solid Professional (valuable in current role; limited growth ceiling) | **Box 2** — High Potential (grow into bigger role; invest) | **Box 1** — Star / Top Talent (promote now; fast track) |
| **Medium Performance** | **Box 7** — Inconsistent (needs coaching; at risk) | **Box 5** — Core Player (steady contributor; maintain) | **Box 3** — Emerging Talent (needs stretch assignments) |
| **Low Performance** | **Box 9** — Underperformer (PIP or exit) | **Box 8** — Mismatch (may improve with training or role change) | **Box 6** — Potential but Unproven (give opportunity but monitor) |

### Box Descriptions

| Box | Label | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Star | Accelerated promotion, mentorship, high-profile projects, retention package |
| **2** | High Potential | Development plan, coaching, leadership programme, broaden exposure |
| **3** | Emerging Talent | Stretch assignments, mentoring, skill building |
| **4** | Solid Professional | Value current contribution, reward, keep engaged |
| **5** | Core Player | Maintain performance, identify growth areas |
| **6** | Potential but Unproven | Give opportunity, set clear goals, monitor closely |
| **7** | Inconsistent | Address specific gaps, set improvement targets |
| **8** | Mismatch | Consider role change, training, or reassessment |
| **9** | Underperformer | PIP or managed exit |

### How to Plot

1. **Performance rating** (from appraisal) → vertical axis
2. **Potential assessment** (from manager + HR + calibration) → horizontal axis
3. Plot each employee on the 3×3 grid.

### Potential Assessment Criteria

| Indicator | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learning agility | Learns new skills rapidly | Learns at average pace | Struggles with new skills |
| Leadership behaviours | Natural leader; influences others | Sometimes leads | Needs direction |
| Career ambition | Clear aspiration to grow | Open to growth but passive | Content in current role |
| Adaptability | Thrives with change | Accepts change | Resists change |
| Problem-solving | Proactive, strategic thinker | Reactive, operational focus | Follows instructions only |

## Talent Review & Calibration

### Calibration Session

**Frequency:** Bi-annual (after mid-year and year-end appraisals).

**Participants:**
- HR Head / HRBP
- Department Heads
- CEO / MD (for senior roles)

**Process:**

```
Each manager presents their team ratings and 9-box placement
           ↓
Cross-department discussion to ensure consistency
           ↓
Adjustments made (up/down) based on evidence
           ↓
Final rating freeze
           ↓
Succession and development actions agreed
```

### Key Discussion Points

| Topic | Questions |
|---|---|
| Rating consistency | "Does 'Meets Expectations' look similar across departments?" |
| Top talent | "Who are our top 10%? What is their development plan?" |
| Risk of loss | "Who might we lose? What is the retention plan?" |
| Gaps | "Which critical roles have no successor?" |
| Diversity | "Are we building a diverse pipeline?" |

|---|---|

| High skill scarcity | Senior Engineer, Data Scientist |

|---|---|---|
| **Ready Now** (1–3 months) | Can step into role immediately | Finalise development, prepare transition |
| **Ready in 1–2 years** | Needs targeted development | Mentorship, stretch assignments, training |

## Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

### When to Use PIP

- Performance rating of **1 (Unsatisfactory)** or repeated rating of **2 (Below Expectations)** .
- Specific, documented performance gaps that have not improved with coaching.
- Behavioural issues (attendance, conduct) that warrant formal improvement.

### PIP Process

```
Manager documents performance gaps with evidence
           ↓
HR reviews and approves PIP initiation
           ↓
Manager holds PIP meeting with employee
           ↓
PIP document signed by both parties
           ↓
Regular check-ins (weekly or bi-weekly)
           ↓
End-of-PIP review and decision
```

### PIP Document Structure

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Performance gaps | Specific, measurable, factual (not subjective) |
| Improvement targets | SMART goals for the PIP period |
| Action plan | Training, coaching, resources provided |
| Check-in schedule | Weekly / bi-weekly |
| Duration | 30–90 days (typical) |
| Consequences | If targets not met: extension, demotion, or termination |

### PIP Outcomes

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Meets all targets | Exit PIP; confirmed in role |
| Partial improvement | Extend PIP (max 1 extension) |
| Does not meet targets | Begin termination process (due process required) |

## Rewards & Recognition

### Performance-Linked Bonus

| Rating | Bonus Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 5 — Outstanding | 1.5 – 2.0× target bonus |
| 4 — Exceeds | 1.0 – 1.5× target bonus |
| 3 — Meets | 1.0× target bonus |
| 2 — Below | 0 – 0.5× target bonus |
| 1 — Unsatisfactory | 0 |

### Merit Increment

| Rating | Merit Increase Guideline |
|---|---|
| 5 — Outstanding | 8–15% |
| 4 — Exceeds | 5–8% |
| 3 — Meets | 3–5% |
| 2 — Below | 0–2% |
| 1 — Unsatisfactory | 0% |

### Promotion Criteria

| Requirement | Guideline |
|---|---|
| Minimum rating | 4 (Exceeds) for at least 2 consecutive cycles |
| 9-box placement | Box 1 or Box 2 |
| Time in grade | At least 12–18 months (varies by level) |
| Successor identified | For the current role |
| Business need | Role vacancy or organisational restructuring |

### Non-Financial Recognition

- Employee of the Month / Quarter
- Shout-outs in town halls or company meetings
- Certificates, plaques, or trophies
- Extra leave days
- Learning and development sponsorship

## Documentation & Record Keeping

### Appraisal Forms Must Include

| Section | Details |
|---|---|
| Employee information | Name, ID, department, title, appraisal period |
| KPI achievement | Each KPI with target vs actual |
| Competency rating | Scored and commented per competency |
| Overall rating | Final rating after calibration |
| Manager comments | Narrative on strengths, areas for improvement |
| Employee comments | Optional: response, rebuttal, or additional input |
| Signatures | Both employee and manager sign (electronic acceptable) |
| Next period goals | KPIs and development goals for the coming period |

### Retention Requirements

| Document | Retention Period |
|---|---|
| Appraisal forms | Duration of employment + 6 years |
| 9-Box Grid placements | 6 years |
| PIP documents | 6 years |
| Calibration session notes | 6 years |
| Rewards recommendations | 6 years |

## Legal Considerations

### Performance-Based Termination

- Termination based on poor performance must follow **due process**.
- Steps:
  1. Document performance gaps.
  2. Issue PIP with clear targets.
  3. Provide support and adequate time.
  4. Conduct end-of-PIP review.
  5. If targets not met, issue show-cause letter.
  6. Hold domestic inquiry (if required).
  7. Terminate with notice OR payment in lieu.

### Wrongful Dismissal Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| No documentation of poor performance | Maintain written records of all issues |
| No PIP before termination | Always issue a PIP first |
| Subjective rather than factual assessment | Use objective KPIs and measurable targets |
| No opportunity to improve | PIP must provide reasonable time and support |
| Discrimination claims | Consistent application of standards across all employees |

### Industrial Relations Act

- Employees with > 12 months of service are protected under the Industrial Relations Act 1967.
- Dismissal must have **just cause and excuse**.
- Employee may file a representation for reinstatement.
- Burden of proof is on the employer.
